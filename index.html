About Me

HIT (Weihai) · Automation

Exploring Autonomous and Intelligent Systems

I am Xiuyi Yang, an undergraduate student in Automation at Harbin Institute of Technology (Weihai). My research interests include automation, unmanned systems, multi-agent systems, trajectory optimization, and embodied intelligence.

I focus on safe control and online trajectory optimization for unmanned systems, with particular interest in Higher-Order Control Barrier Functions (HOCBF), Quadratic Programming (QP), and Model Predictive Path Integral control (MPPI).

I am also exploring multi-agent coordination and World Models (WM), aiming to connect environment modeling, prediction, planning, and decision-making for embodied agents.

Research Outputs

Selected work in unmanned systems, safety control, and energy management

01
CCDC 2027 Submitted

Initial Formation Allocation for Unmanned Swarms Based on Predictive Safety-Control Intervention Degree

Abstract

Conventional formation assignment treats geometric distance as a proxy for the quality of a mapping. That proxy is misleading when agents have different dynamics, paths interact, and the controller must intervene to maintain safety. We introduce a one-shot method that enumerates agent–target bijections and scores each using obstacle-aware geometry and conflict, predicted HOCBF-QP (High-Order Control Barrier Function Quadratic Program) intervention, and task-progress loss. A candidate must also pass diagnostic feasibility and finite-interval clearance tests; if none does, an obstacle-aware Hungarian assignment is used. The selected mapping is then fixed and executed by a hard HOCBF-QP. In five scenarios, the method improves joint success over four baselines. Paired ablations separate the roles of intervention scoring and interval screening: the former affects ranking and progress, while the latter acts as an independent safety gate.

Keywordsunmanned swarm; formation reconfiguration; target allocation; control barrier function; predictive safety-control intervention
02
PS-LAF 2026 Accepted

Battery-Aware Distributed Power Allocation for Low-Altitude UAV Fleets

Abstract

Low-altitude UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) fleets must share mission-power demand while accommodating unequal battery capacity, state of charge (SoC), state of health (SoH), and safety reserves. This study develops a distributed supervisory allocator whose commands are executed locally by each aircraft, without inter-aircraft energy transfer. To address the inability of conventional incremental-cost consensus to represent usable reserve and preserve allocation after planned replacement, we introduce a capacity- and SoH-aware reserve coefficient, a projected surplus-based iteration, and a command-plus-surplus handoff for acknowledged one-for-one replacement. The method is evaluated on an IRIS+-scale six-UAV benchmark under varying graph topologies, packet loss, membership changes, and paired missions. The distributed solution differs from the centralized reference by less than 2.5 × 10−8, and local handoff recovers within 100 iterations in 95.6% of 500 events. Across 500 paired 7-min missions, success reaches 64.2%, compared with 62.0% for SoC-only allocation; the reserve-time gain is 0.067 min (95% bootstrap interval: 0.057–0.078 min) and rises to 0.191 min at 30% capacity CV (Coefficient of Variation). These results support a practical battery-aware supervisory layer for low-altitude multi-UAV operations.

Keywordslow-altitude UAV fleet; distributed power allocation; battery capacity; state of charge; state of health; replacement handoff
